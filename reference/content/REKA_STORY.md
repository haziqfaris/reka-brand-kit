## The story in chapters

### 1. 2013: a design studio
REKA started in 2013 as REKA Studios, a graphics and web design company working with Malaysian businesses.

### 2. 2014 to 2015: learning by teaching
In 2014 the studio moved its focus to technology. Through 2014 and 2015 the team ran a lot of hands-on training:
- web development
- electronics on Raspberry Pi and Arduino
- the Internet of Things
- mobile apps built with PhoneGap and AppDroid

Teaching others meant mastering each tool first. That breadth of hardware plus software became the base for everything that followed.

### 3. 2016: early AI
In 2016 the team moved into early artificial intelligence:
- convolutional neural networks (CNN) for images
- optimisation
- image processing
- digital signal processing (DSP)

### 4. 2016: Malaysia's first self-driving car
- **Start:** in September 2016 REKA began building its own self-driving technology.
- **Kit:** a retrofit kit for an ordinary car, run by a controller board REKA designed itself, called RIG (Reka Innovation Gear), with off-the-shelf cameras, radar, laser and GPS.
- **First test:** by the end of 2016 the car drove between Kuala Lumpur and Melaka with no one in the driver's seat.
- **Name:** REKA called the project CRETA.

Suggested REKA wording: "In 2016 we built Malaysia's first self-driving car." This is REKA's own claim, so do not use it on AntaraX.

### 5. 2017: REKA Inisiatif, and the public takes a ride
- In 2017 the studio became REKA Inisiatif Sdn Bhd, an R&D company spanning AI, sensing and autonomous systems.
- At the Malaysia Autoshow 2017 the public rode in its self-driving car.

### 6. 2018 to 2021: from cars to software automation
The car worked. Bringing it to market was another matter. Across the region the roadblocks were:
- commercialisation
- regulation
- road and network infrastructure
- market adoption
- culture

So from 2018 to 2021 REKA put its main effort into software automation instead of developing autonomous cars further.

The autonomous-vehicle work never stopped:
- REKA built government ties across Southeast Asia.
- It developed frameworks for deploying autonomous vehicles quickly in different situations.
- It made this an ongoing initiative to raise awareness and to clear the way for new and existing autonomous-vehicle startups in the region.

### 7. The pandemic: keeping companies running remotely
- **What REKA did:** when the pandemic closed workplaces, REKA turned seriously to automation, robotic process automation (RPA) and robotics, to help organisations keep operating while people worked remotely.
- **Who it helped:** UMW Industries and Murata Electronics, among others, which used this work to run tasks remotely.
- **What it taught:** most operational work still needs people. The value comes from seeing every task live and letting a few people steer many. That lesson later became Yara.

### 8. Sensing the city
- REKA's sensing work, rooted in the car's sensors and edge computing, went into smart-city sensing with Murata.
- It also went into crowd analytics across Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam.
- That work was recognised by Frost & Sullivan. The owner's wording: say "recognised", not "award".
- The line continues as PLExyz.

### 9. Partners and the open-source stack
- 2022: an MoU with Elmlab to co-develop autonomous-vehicle technology, covering connectivity and teleoperation.
- 2023: an autonomous test bed shown at the Malaysia Autoshow.
- 2024: REKA joined the Autoware Foundation, after building on the open-source Autoware stack since 2021.

### 10. 2026: AntaraX, and research that continues
- In 2026 REKA spun its proven, repeatable technology into AntaraX, a product company with one platform underneath, Lapisan.
- The full autonomy stack stays at REKA, in its Kinetik division, where autonomous machines are still being built.
