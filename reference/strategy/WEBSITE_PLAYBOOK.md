## 1. Positioning

**Category:** Technology R&D & Innovation Partner.

REKA is not primarily a software house, coding company, generic AI consultancy, training company or catalogue of technologies.

**Core philosophy:** Technology is not the outcome. Progress is.

**Commercial expression:** You make more money. Move faster. Build what others can't.

Supporting outcomes: spend smarter, reduce risk, create new capability, gain an advantage, see what comes next.

If copy could belong unchanged to a generic software agency, rewrite it.

## 2. What REKA sells

Code, AI, robotics, autonomous systems, sensing, hardware, software, advanced computing and quantum are tools.

REKA sells perspective, problem solving, technological advantage, R&D capability, experimentation, validation, implementation, capability creation and measurable progress.

Start with: **What are you trying to make possible?**

Do not start with: **Where can we put AI?**

## 3. Story and credibility

REKA began in 2013 as a builder across coding, design and web. In 2016 autonomous-vehicle work forced deeper capability in AI, perception, sensing, mapping, data, real-time systems, software, hardware and physical machines. That multidisciplinary R&D capability was then applied across industries.

Two principles follow:

- Technology agnostic.
- Industry agnostic.

REKA should continue exploring what comes next, including robotics, autonomous systems, advanced computing and quantum, without pretending every frontier technology is already a commercial answer.

**AI isn't always the final answer.**

## 4. The REKA advantage

### See what others don't
Find missed opportunity, value leakage, inefficient processes, outdated assumptions and technical possibilities.

### Connect what others don't
Bring lessons across industries, sciences and technologies.

### Prove before committing
Not every exciting idea deserves investment. A valid result can be: **Don't build it.**

### Build what doesn't exist
Research, prototype, validate, engineer and integrate where off-the-shelf answers are insufficient.

### Own the outcome
Do not disappear after producing a strategy document.

## 6. Existing teams and vendors

If the customer already has a technology team: good.

> **Your team knows your company from the inside. We bring what we've learned everywhere else.**

> **Don't outsource your development. Extend what your company is capable of.**

If they already use AI: even better. Find where AI creates measurable value, where it integrates, what can be trusted and where AI should not be used.

If they already have developers/vendors: keep them. REKA can find the opportunity, challenge assumptions, prove the approach, define what should be built and work alongside the existing team.

If they already have an AI/technology strategy: **Then let's build it.**

## 7. Method

# SEE → PROVE → BUILD → ADVANCE

**SEE — Find what is worth changing.**
Understand objectives, revenue, friction, cost, systems, capability, customer experience and missed opportunity. Output can be an Opportunity Map.

**PROVE — Find out what is worth building.**
Test technical feasibility, commercial value, data readiness, integration, adoption, risk and time-to-value. Outputs can include experiments, prototypes, feasibility work and value models.

**BUILD — Make it real.**
Architecture, engineering, AI, automation, sensing, integration, robotics, autonomous systems, interfaces, deployment and production hardening.

**ADVANCE — Make it useful and better over time.**
Adoption, operational integration, monitoring, improvement, scaling, training, knowledge transfer and next-stage opportunity identification.

Core line: **Bespoke implementation. Proven framework.**

## 8. Strategic project fit

REKA should not become a high-volume project shop.

A strong engagement should ideally create at least two of:
- meaningful commercial value;
- strategic customer advantage;
- reusable REKA know-how;
- repeatable components/frameworks;
- future product potential;
- long-term/recurring opportunity.

> **Fewer problems. More meaningful problems.**

## 9. Brand breadth vs GTM focus

Brand: **industry agnostic, technology agnostic.**

GTM: deliberately focused campaigns.

Relevant campaign territories can include professional services, manufacturing, property/commercial buildings, logistics/supply chain, mobility, infrastructure and selected oil & gas opportunities.

Do not turn campaign verticals into REKA's permanent identity.

## 11. Flagship conversion

# What Are You Not Seeing?
**Free 3-minute Opportunity Assessment**

Ask what the visitor wants to achieve and where they are today. Return a small number of opportunities, including something they probably do not need. Then invite them to an Opportunity Session.

Useful supporting tools can include AI ROI, automation ROI, build-vs-partner, AI readiness, technology waste, revenue opportunity, AI agent fit, robotics feasibility and quantum fit.

Tools must provide value; they must not be disguised contact forms.

## 12. Proof architecture

Every important project should eventually be captured as:

**Problem → What REKA saw → What we did → Why the obvious answer wasn't enough → Technology used → Result → Business impact → What we learned**

Provide a simple Business View and, where useful, a deeper Technical View.

> **Show what we achieved. Let people inspect how we did it.**

## 16. SEO / AEO / GEO

Maintain four search territories:
- **OWN** — queries REKA already owns.
- **STEAL** — valuable territory competitors dominate.
- **GAP** — high-intent questions competitors answer poorly.
- **EMERGING** — demand beginning to grow.

Create canonical answer assets with:
Direct answer → explanation → decision framework → economics → examples → alternatives → FAQ → proof → sources → assessment/tool.

Potential emerging themes include physical/embodied AI, agent reliability, hybrid AI, inference economics, AI-to-robot workflows, quantum optimization readiness and quantum + AI. Publish only where REKA has something defensible to say.

## 17. Visual character

REKA should feel curious, exploratory, experienced, multidisciplinary, engineered, ambitious and human.

Prefer real machines, prototypes, labs, people building, autonomous systems, robotics, sensing, diagrams and technical artifacts.

Avoid generic SaaS visuals, giant technology-logo walls, stock-photo digital transformation and unsupported AI spectacle.

## 18. REKA as evidence

REKA should use AI, automation and emerging technology internally where they genuinely improve research, development, testing, delivery, knowledge, reporting, marketing, sales enablement, support and operations.

Public principle: **We use internally what we recommend externally — where it genuinely works.**

## 19. AntaraX boundary

REKA: Explore → Research → Prototype → Engineer → Validate.

When capability becomes proven and repeatable, it may move toward AntaraX: Productize → Deploy → Operate → Scale.

**Graduation rule.** A capability moves to AntaraX when it has a repeatable deployment, a published starting price and a fast path to first value. Until then it stays with REKA. Robotics and autonomy stay with REKA's Kinetik division; they are not AntaraX products.

Example REKA already states publicly: EXP-04 became PLExyz (now AntaraX's Edge family). Add a Yara example only once its origin story is confirmed.

REKA does not sell PLExyz, Tinara or AntaraX products. They are linked as products that came from REKA's research.

If an AntaraX product already solves most of a customer's problem, do not custom-build a duplicate through REKA.

Public bridge:
> **When research becomes repeatable. Some problems we've solved repeatedly deserve to become products. That's why we created AntaraX.**

## 20. Global position

Design REKA as global-first. Malaysia is origin and proof, not market limitation. Avoid making “Malaysia's leading...” the core global proposition.

## 21. Implementation rule

When changing the site:
1. Preserve the positioning above unless explicitly asked to change strategy.
2. Prefer outcome language before technology language.
3. Show proof before claims.
4. Keep top-level copy simple; put technical depth on dedicated pages.
5. Do not invent customers, metrics, awards, partnerships, case-study results or technical claims.
6. Preserve existing working functionality unless the task requires changing it.
7. Treat conversion, SEO, AEO/GEO, accessibility, responsiveness and performance as product requirements, not afterthoughts.
8. Use real REKA work wherever available.
9. Before major copy/IA changes, check this file again.
10. If implementation constraints conflict with this strategy, surface the conflict rather than silently changing the positioning.
