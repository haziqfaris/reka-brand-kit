# 04 Voice and claims

## Voice

REKA sounds **experienced, curious, precise and quietly confident**: an engineer who has seen many industries and is unimpressed by buzzwords. More technical than AntaraX, but the top layer must still be understood by a non-technical executive in seconds; depth goes one layer down.

- Outcome language before technology language.
- Proof before claims.
- Name the situation the reader is in ("I know AI matters, but not where to start").
- Say no when no is right. "Don't build it" is a valid result.
- Short, declarative lines; a dry turn of phrase is welcome; hype is not.

Reference lines:

- Built for Progress.
- Technology is not the outcome. Progress is.
- You make more money. Move faster. Build what others can't.
- Whichever door, we sell return, not software.
- AI, run for you.
- Your team knows your company from the inside. We bring what we've learned everywhere else.
- Don't outsource your development. Extend what your company is capable of.
- Fewer problems. More meaningful problems.
- We use internally what we recommend externally, where it genuinely works.

## Existing teams and vendors

If a customer already has a team, AI, vendors or a strategy: good. REKA extends, challenges, proves and works alongside; it never asks them to replace what works.

## Style

- Sentence case for headings and buttons. Section markers in lower-case mono with `//` ("// how it works").
- British English. No em dashes in customer copy.
- Numbers and labels in JetBrains Mono.
- Exclamation marks only for confirmed critical alerts.

## Claims rules (never break)

1. Never invent customers, metrics, awards, partnerships, case-study results, research claims or security claims.
2. Use only facts marked confirmed in [03](03-story-and-facts.md). Named customers need permission.
3. Frost & Sullivan: "recognised", never "award".
4. Do not present frontier technology (quantum, full autonomy) as an available commercial answer when it is research.
5. Do not sell AntaraX products as REKA products; link to them.
6. Case studies follow: Problem → What REKA saw → What we did → Why the obvious answer wasn't enough → Technology used → Result → Business impact → What we learned. A simple business view, a deeper technical view where useful.

## Avoid

Generic agency phrases ("digital transformation", "end-to-end solutions", "cutting-edge"), "Malaysia's leading…" as a proposition, giant technology-logo walls, unsupported AI spectacle.
