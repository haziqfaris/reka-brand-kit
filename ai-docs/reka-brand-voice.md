# REKA Brand Voice & Copy Rules
> Use this file when writing any REKA copy: web, decks, reports, UI text.
> Updated: June 2026

## Positioning statements (use verbatim)
- **Primary:** "AI for the physical world."
- **Supporting:** "Born on Southeast Asian roads. Built for global operations."
- **Mission:** "Enable people to create."

## Tone
| Attribute | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Precise** | Every word earns its place. Cut filler. If it serves no purpose, remove it. |
| **Inventive** | New framing for hard ideas. Avoid the familiar phrase when a better one exists. |
| **Grounded** | Honest about what works. No inflated claims, no theatre. |
| **Forward** | Building the next decade. Avoid "catching up" language. |
| **Quietly confident** | The work speaks. The copy supports it, not the other way around. |

## Hard rules

### No em dashes
❌ `AI for the physical world — not just screens.`
✅ `AI for the physical world, not just screens.`
✅ `AI for the physical world (not just screens).`

### No currency in value claims
❌ `Reduced costs by RM 2 million.`
✅ `Reduced operational cost by 40%.` *(if verified)*
✅ `Reduced operational cost by 2× (illustrative).` *(if illustrative)*

### Always flag illustrative metrics
✅ `Inference latency reduced by 40% (illustrative figure).`

### Claim hierarchy
- ✅ "Malaysia's first self-driving car on open roads" (2016, CRETA)
- ❌ "Southeast Asia's first" — not defensible
- ✅ "One of the very few independent commercial R&D companies in Southeast Asia building AI for the physical world"

### Titles
- ✅ Haziq Faris, **Founder**
- ❌ Haziq Faris, CEO

### No off-the-shelf framing
❌ "We refuse off-the-shelf solutions."
✅ "We begin at the root of the problem and build the answer to fit it."

## Preferred phrases
- "Start at the root" — how REKA approaches problems
- "Honest engineering" — no overstated results, no theatre
- "Built for the real world" — if it can't survive a road, a crowd, or a factory floor, it isn't done
- "Grounded, then global" — Southeast Asian roots, world-class output

## What to avoid
- Superlatives without evidence ("world-class", "industry-leading", "best-in-class")
- Jargon without explanation
- Passive voice in headlines
- Promises of specific future performance
- Emojis in formal materials
